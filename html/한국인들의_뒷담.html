한국인들의 뒷담

How Koreans Talk Behind Their Back — a learner’s breakdown of the key expressions (뒷담화, 가식 떨다, 철벽 치다, 나대다, 이간질, 손민수) and the grammar around them, with the podcast’s argument that shared gossip strengthens couples.

Host: 챌시  •  Level: Intermediate (TOPIK ~3–4)  •  Style: 해요체 / 합쇼체 narration, 반말 in dialogues

01 What is 뒷담화?

다른 사람이 없는 자리에서, 뒤에서 그 사람에 대해서 수군수군 안 좋게 나쁘게 말하는 걸 뒷담화 한다고 하는데요.
Dareun sarami eomneun jarieseo, dwieseo geu sarame daehaeseo sugunsugun an joke nappeuge malhaneun geol dwitdamhwa handago haneundeyo.
"Speaking ill of someone in a whisper, behind their back, where they aren’t present — that’s what we call ‘doing 뒷담화’."
KoreanRomanizationPart of speechGloss
뒷담화dwitdamhwaNoun (뒤 + 談話)gossip; badmouthing
뒷담 깐다dwitdam kkandaColloquial phraseto badmouth (까다 = to peel/shell)
수군수군sugunsugunMimetic adverbin whispers
자리jariNounplace; setting; occasion
나쁘게nappeugeAdverb (-게)badly; negatively
Grammar: -(이)라고 하다 names a thing: 뒷담화 한다고 하는데요 = “[it] is called doing 뒷담화.” -는 걸 is the casual contraction of -는 것을 (nominalizer + object particle). -는데요 ends a clause softly and invites the listener to continue — very common in podcasts. -게 turns adjectives into adverbs (나쁘다 → 나쁘게). 안 좋게 + 나쁘게 stacked is for emphasis.

02 How Gossip Starts (Red Flags)

오해하지 말고 들어... 어디 가서 나한테 들었다고 말하면 안 돼, 알았지?
Ohaehaji malgo deureo... Eodi gaseo nahante deureotdago malhamyeon an dwae, aratji?
"Don’t get me wrong, listen... Don’t go telling anyone you heard it from me, okay?"
KoreanRomanizationPart of speechGloss
근데 있잖아geunde itjanaAttention-getterso, you know what...
이런 말 하면 좀 그렇지만ireon mal hamyeon jom geureochimanHedge phraseit’s awkward to say, but...
오해하다ohaehadaVerbto misunderstand
들었다고deureotdagoQuoted past (듣다)[that you] heard
레드 플래그redeu peullaegeuLoanwordred flag; warning sign
Grammar: -지 말고 = “don’t do X, [do Y instead]” (오해하지 말고 들어). -(으)면 안 돼 = prohibition. -았/었다고 말하다 is an indirect quote of a past event. 듣다 is a ㄷ-irregular (들어, 들었다). Note 좀 그렇지만: 그렇다 stands in for something unsaid — a classic Korean euphemism. The host switches to 합쇼체 for the joke: 피하십시오 (“please avoid”).

03 호박씨 까다, 가식 떨다 & the 떨다 Pattern

'가식 떨다'에서 '하다'라고 안 하고 이렇게 '떨다'라고 했잖아요.
‘Gasik tteolda’eseo ‘hada’go an hago ireoke ‘tteolda’go haetjanayo.
"In ‘가식 떨다,’ we don’t say ‘하다’ — we say ‘떨다,’ remember?"
KoreanRomanizationPart of speechGloss
뒤에서 호박씨 까다dwieseo hobakssi kkadaProverbto act innocent in front, scheme behind (lit. crack pumpkin seeds)
앞뒤가 다르다apdwiga dareudaIdiomto be two-faced
가식 (假飾)gasikSino-Korean nounpretense; false front
엄살 떨다eomsal tteoldaN + 떨다to exaggerate pain
호들갑 떨다hodeulgap tteoldaN + 떨다to make a fuss; overreact
수다 떨다suda tteoldaN + 떨다to chat (the neutral one!)
유난스럽다yunanseureopdaㅂ-irregular adj.over-the-top; fussy
-ㄴ/은 척-n/eun cheokPatternpretending to be (착한 척, 아픈 척)
Grammar: Noun + 떨다 is a light-verb pattern like 하다, but it adds a nuance of excessive, showy behavior. Only 수다 떨다 is neutral. 떨다 here is not “to tremble” — the host says so explicitly. -잖아요 = “as you know / remember” (했잖아요, 있잖아요). -아/어 가지고 (시켜 가지고, 되가지고) is a spoken connector meaning “and so / because.” -ㄴ/은 척 하다 = pretend. 호박씨 is a proverb (속담) — learn it as a chunk.
누가 자기한테 호감을 표현했을 때 여지를 주지 않고 단호하게 딱 거절하는 걸 '철벽 친다'고 하거든요.
Nuga jagihante hogameul pyohyeonhaesseul ttae yeojireul juji anko danhohage ttak geojeolhaneun geol ‘cheolbyeok chinda’go hageodeunyo.
"When someone shows interest in you and you refuse firmly, giving no opening at all — that’s called ‘hitting an iron wall’."
KoreanRomanizationPart of speechGloss
철벽 치다cheolbyeok chidaSlang phraseto shut down romantic advances
철벽남 / 철벽녀cheolbyeongnam / -nyeoNounguy / girl who is unapproachable
여지yeojiNounroom; leeway; opening
단호하게danhohageAdverbresolutely
플러팅peulleotingLoanwordflirting
Grammar: -거든요 gives background or a reason the listener doesn’t know yet (‘you see, it’s called...’). -았/었을 때 = “when [it happened].” -지 않고 = “without doing.” Pronunciation: 철벽남 is said [철벼-ㅁ남] with nasalization (ㄱ→ㅇ before ㄴ): cheolbyeongnam.

04 나대다 — “The Final Form of Korean Gossip”

나댄다는 거는 기본적으로 내가 나설 상황이 아닌데 선을 넘고 계속 행동하는 거죠.
Nadaendaneun geoneun gibonjeogeuro naega naseol sanghwangi anindae seoneul neomgo gyesok haengdonghaneun geojyo.
"‘나대다’ basically means continuing to act, crossing the line, in a situation where it’s not your place to step forward."
KoreanRomanizationPart of speechGloss
나대다nadaedaVerb (slang)to show off; overstep; act too pushy
나서다naseodaVerbto step forward; get involved
선을 넘다seoneul neomdaIdiomto cross the line
참견하다chamgyeonhadaVerbto meddle
아는 척 / 잘난 척aneun cheok / jallan cheokPatternacting knowledgeable / superior
튀다twidaVerbto stand out (negatively)
똑 부러지다ttok bureojidaAdjective-like verbsharp and decisive (positive)
찝찝하다jjipjjiphadaAdjectiveuneasy; icky; unsettled
Grammar: The key insight: 나대다 is subjective — the same behavior is “confident” (적극적이다) to a fan and 나댄다 to someone who dislikes the person. Forms to learn: 왜 저렇게 나대? (“why is [he/she] so pushy?”), 나대지 마 (“don’t show off”), 적당히 좀 나대라 (“tone it down”). Used on yourself: 내가 너무 나댔나? — -았/었나? is a self-directed “did I...?” question. -(으)ㄹ 상황이 아니다 = “it isn’t a situation to...”. Cultural note: the word itself shows that standing out is something Koreans often try to avoid.

05 이간질 & 손민수

'질'은 좋지 않은 행동을 약간 낮춰서 말할 때 쓰는 글자예요.
‘Jil’eun joji anheun haengdongeul yakgan natchwoseo malhal ttae sseuneun geuljayeyo.
"‘질’ is a syllable used to talk about bad behavior in a somewhat belittling way."
KoreanRomanizationPart of speechGloss
이간질 (離間-)iganjilNounsowing discord between people
-질-jilNoun suffix[derogatory] act of
도둑질 / 싸움질dodukjil / ssaumjilNounstealing / brawling
손가락질 / 정치질songarakjil / jeongchijilNounpointing fingers / political scheming
손민수(하다)sonminsu(hada)Slang verbto copy someone’s style or items
손민수템sonminsutemSlang nounitem bought to match an idol/influencer
최애choeaeNounone’s favorite (member)
굳어지다gudeojidaVerbto become fixed (as a word)
Grammar: Noun + 질 + 하다 forms insulting activity nouns; compare the neutral 도둑 (thief) vs. 도둑질 (the act). 이간질 하고 다니다 = “go around sowing discord” (-고 다니다 = habitually do while going about). 손민수 comes from a character in the drama 치즈 인 더 트랩 who copied the lead’s style — a rare case of a name turning into a verb. In the dialogue: -(ㄴ/는)다나 뭐라나 = “or something like that,” with a dismissive, gossipy tone (손민수하는 것 같다나 뭐라나).

06 The Argument: Shared Dislikes Make Couples Last

좋아하는 취향은 다를 수 있다. 근데 반대로 싫어하는 거에 대한 취향이 비슷해야 더 오래 가는 것 같아요.
Joahaneun chwihyangeun dareul su itda. Geunde bandaero sirheohaneun geoe daehan chwihyangi biseushaeya deo orae ganeun geot gatayo.
"Tastes in what you like can differ. But conversely, it seems you last longer if what you dislike is similar."
KoreanRomanizationPart of speechGloss
갈구다galgudaSlang verbto harass; ride someone
털어놓다teoreonotaVerbto vent; confide
유대감yudaegamNounsense of bonding
돈독해지다dondokhaejidaVerbto grow deeper (of ties)
공감대gonggamdaeNouncommon ground; shared feeling
가치관gachigwanNounvalues; worldview
균열gyunyeolNouncrack; rift
Grammar: -아/어야 + -(으)ㄹ 것 같다 (비슷해야 더 오래 가는 것 같아요) = “it seems that only if... will it last.” -(으)ㄹ 수 있다 / 없을 수는 없잖아요 is a double negative: “it’s impossible for there to be none.” -ㄴ다고 치다 (좋아한다고 쳐요) = “suppose that...”. 에 대한 = “regarding.” 반대로 = “conversely,” a handy discourse marker for opinions.

07 Key Patterns at a Glance

1. Noun + 떨다 — showy behavior (가식, 엄살, 호들갑; 수다 is neutral).
2. -ㄴ/은 척 하다 — pretending (착한 척, 아는 척).
3. Noun + 질 — derogatory act (이간질, 도둑질, 손가락질).
4. -잖아요 / -거든요 — shared knowledge vs. new background reason.
5. -지 말고 / -지 마 — “don’t” commands in casual gossip.
6. -(ㄴ/는)다나 뭐라나 — dismissive “or whatever” reporting.
7. -아/어 가지고 — spoken connector (“and so”).
